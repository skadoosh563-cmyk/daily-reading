## 一、导读

文章讨论美国中期选举民主党大胜对欧洲的影响。短期看，特朗普在国内受制后可能更肆意外交，对欧洲更对抗，欧洲处境未必改善。中期看，民主党掌控国会未必会替欧洲出面，民族主义民粹政党也未必因与特朗普关联而受损。长期看，美国民主的韧性可能给欧洲进步力量带来希望，激励主流政党回应青年诉求。文章逻辑层层递进，适合学习外刊常见政治评论表达。

## 二、原文

“Is Maga dead?” asked Margaret, a Guardian reader in Switzerland, in a live Q&A with our correspondents after the seismic US midterm elections. If Margaret’s query expressed a hope that the Democrats’ blue wave could transform Europe’s fortunes after two years of Maga-induced trauma, she was probably not alone.

The resounding defeat for Donald Trump’s Republicans and the installation of a Democratic-controlled US Congress has been met with understandable relief in European capitals. In the short term however, the worst for Europe is not over. Trump didn’t need congressional approval, after all, to unleash a trade war on his supposed European allies, threaten to invade Greenland, try to stitch up Ukraine or go to war with Iran, an act that has inflated fuel and food prices disastrously.

Hemmed in now on domestic policy, the US president may, many European leaders fear, use his extensive foreign policy powers to become even more confrontational on the world stage. Europe, warned Nathalie Tocci, is exactly where Trump will lash out. “Trump and Maga have always seen Europe as the mirror image of their domestic foes: a bunch of presumptuous, woke, emasculated liberals. Now that those foes have made a comeback at home, Trump’s rancour towards Europe is likely to grow.”

The lashing out could come in the form of new tariff chaos. As the humiliated Maga figurehead (one Washington expert likened the movement now to “a cornered ferret”), Trump could veto aid for Ukraine and thwart Kyiv’s access to defence technology. Paul Taylor sketched a fearful scenario in which he could even attempt a carve-up of eastern Europe in the so-called “big, beautiful deal” for Ukraine that he says he is working on with Russia. The omens for Europe were poor as US-mediated talks opened in Abu Dhabi, with delegates from Germany, France, the UK and the EU, literally sidelined.

Nor can Europe rely on the new intake of congressional Democrats to challenge Trump on its behalf, Blanche Leridon of the Institut Montaigne thinktank in Paris, tells me. “We have to be clear-sighted that the Democrats’ priority in Congress won’t be to re-establish a more harmonious relationship with Europe. Their foreign policy is, at this point, difficult to decipher. There isn’t a single, consistent position between the more progressive and centrist factions when it comes to either Europe or Ukraine specifically.”

We can’t even assume that nationalist populist parties, encouraged by Trump to destroy the EU from the inside, will suffer by their association with him. True, in central and eastern Europe, where the Maga spirit was heavily promoted by Viktor Orbán, some incumbents may have to perform a pivot. Poland’s conservative president Karol Nawrocki, who is backed by the opposition Eurosceptic Law and Justice party and has vaunted his proximity to Trump, is now facing awkward questions. And in Czechia, which has one of the most pro-Maga governments in Europe, populist PM Andrej Babiš and his far-right coalition partners, the Motorists party, have faced a wave of grassroots protests lately.

In Italy meanwhile, the far-right prime minister Giorgia Meloni played up tensions with Trump months ago, a strategic move to allay the concerns of voters, the majority of whom despise him.

But in countries where populists have not yet been in government and are polling at record levels, Trump’s humiliation may not matter to voters, even if their grievances – such as stagnant pay, inflation and lack of affordable housing – are often driven by global forces.

“It would be wrong to conclude that national-populist and reactionary forces are in retreat everywhere in the west,” Leridon says. “The Democrats won in the US because people were fed up with Trump, in Serbia, [Aleksandar] Vučić was overthrown after 14 years, but in countries where populist political forces are not in power, but on the verge of gaining it, the calculation is different.”

In France, Marine Le Pen is still leading in the polls, six months ahead of a presidential election being fought on cost-of-living issues. Her far-right National Rally party identified Trump as a liability some time ago. “Le Pen and [Jordan] Bardella understood perfectly the radioactive effect the US president could have on them as politicians claiming to defend French national interests. Above all, they recoiled from the corrosive impact of Trump’s policies on purchasing power in France, whether via trade tariffs or the war in Iran,” Leridon says.

In Germany, Alice Weidel, a co-leader of the far-right Alternative für Deutschland, had also distanced the party from Trump in response to tariffs and Greenland. Weidel now blames Trump’s defeat on broken promises about going to war with Iran.

A critical test for Europe’s political direction is looming in Spain. Socialist prime minister Pedro Sánchez, who called a snap election for 29 November, welcomed Trump’s defeat as a straw in the wind for the revival of progressive forces. But amid public outcry over housing and wages (the cause of Wednesday’s general strike), the momentum is with the opposition rightwing Popular Party (PP) – which could coalesce with the far-right Vox. If this happens, it will be the first time a far-right party enters government in Spain since the end of the Franco dictatorship in 1975. Santiago Abascal, the Vox leader, dismissed any suggestion of an ideological connection with Trump’s Republicans. “We are in Spain, not in Michigan,” he said after the midterms.

A yearning for generational change

And yet, longer term, the broader, more hopeful lesson from across the Atlantic could be that after years of global backsliding liberal democracy is not dead. “If Europe can celebrate an essential point, it is that American democracy is alive and was the big winner of these elections,” Leridon says.

Could this even, as Alexander Hurst asked, be a “democratic spring” – one that began in Budapest in April with Orbán’s defeat? Julian Borger unpacked how a sequence of four pivotal elections this year, in Hungary, Serbia, Brazil and now the US, has shown the power of grassroots activism to produce surprises and could, perhaps, begin the reversal of a worldwide drift towards authoritarianism.

The US vote could even shake Europe’s ailing mainstream parties out of their fatalistic mimicking of far-right policies and into action. Gen Z voters fuelled the US blue wave, which showed a yearning for generational change. At a time when youth-led protest movements have rocked Europe, progressive parties may be able to channel protest energy into serious political change. They can’t say they haven’t been warned, loudly, to heed calls for democratic renewal, education, decent housing, equality and limits on the power of the big tech billionaires. Or that failure to do so could allow grassroots anger to be co-opted into the same kind of anti-system nihilism that fuelled Maga.

## 三、逐段翻译

**1.** “Is Maga dead?” asked Margaret, a Guardian reader in Switzerland, in a live Q&A with our correspondents after the seismic US midterm elections. If Margaret’s query expressed a hope that the Democrats’ blue wave could transform Europe’s fortunes after two years of Maga-induced trauma, she was probably not alone.

“Maga死了吗？”瑞士的《卫报》读者玛格丽特在具有重大影响的美国中期选举后，在我们的记者直播问答中问道。如果玛格丽特的提问表达了一种希望，即民主党的蓝色浪潮能在两年Maga带来的创伤后改变欧洲的命运，那么她很可能并不孤单。

**2.** The resounding defeat for Donald Trump’s Republicans and the installation of a Democratic-controlled US Congress has been met with understandable relief in European capitals. In the short term however, the worst for Europe is not over. Trump didn’t need congressional approval, after all, to unleash a trade war on his supposed European allies, threaten to invade Greenland, try to stitch up Ukraine or go to war with Iran, an act that has inflated fuel and food prices disastrously.

唐纳德·特朗普所属共和党的惨败以及民主党控制的美国国会的成立，在欧洲各国首都引发了可以理解的宽慰。然而短期内，欧洲最糟糕的时刻尚未过去。毕竟，特朗普不需要国会批准就对所谓的欧洲盟友发动贸易战、威胁入侵格陵兰、试图摆布乌克兰或与伊朗开战，而这些行为已灾难性地推高了燃料和食品价格。

**3.** Hemmed in now on domestic policy, the US president may, many European leaders fear, use his extensive foreign policy powers to become even more confrontational on the world stage. Europe, warned Nathalie Tocci, is exactly where Trump will lash out. “Trump and Maga have always seen Europe as the mirror image of their domestic foes: a bunch of presumptuous, woke, emasculated liberals. Now that those foes have made a comeback at home, Trump’s rancour towards Europe is likely to grow.”

许多欧洲领导人担心，如今在国内政策上受到制约的美国总统，可能会利用其广泛的外交政策权力，在世界舞台上变得更加对抗。娜塔莉·托奇警告说，欧洲正是特朗普将猛烈抨击的地方。“特朗普和Maga一直把欧洲视为其国内敌人的镜像：一群自以为是、觉醒、被阉割的自由派。既然这些敌人在国内卷土重来，特朗普对欧洲的怨恨可能会加深。”

**4.** The lashing out could come in the form of new tariff chaos. As the humiliated Maga figurehead (one Washington expert likened the movement now to “a cornered ferret”), Trump could veto aid for Ukraine and thwart Kyiv’s access to defence technology. Paul Taylor sketched a fearful scenario in which he could even attempt a carve-up of eastern Europe in the so-called “big, beautiful deal” for Ukraine that he says he is working on with Russia. The omens for Europe were poor as US-mediated talks opened in Abu Dhabi, with delegates from Germany, France, the UK and the EU literally sidelined.

这种猛烈抨击可能以新的关税混乱形式出现。作为受辱的Maga名义领袖（一位华盛顿专家现在将该运动比作“被逼入绝境的雪貂”），特朗普可能会否决对乌克兰的援助，并阻挠基辅获得国防技术。保罗·泰勒描绘了一个可怕的情景：特朗普甚至可能试图在他所称正与俄罗斯推进的所谓“宏大而美好的协议”中瓜分东欧。随着美国斡旋的会谈在阿布扎比开始，来自德国、法国、英国和欧盟的代表被完全排除在外，这对欧洲来说是不祥之兆。

**5.** Nor can Europe rely on the new intake of congressional Democrats to challenge Trump on its behalf, Blanche Leridon of the Institut Montaigne thinktank in Paris, tells me. “We have to be clear-sighted that the Democrats’ priority in Congress won’t be to re-establish a more harmonious relationship with Europe. Their foreign policy is, at this point, difficult to decipher. There isn’t a single, consistent position between the more progressive and centrist factions when it comes to either Europe or Ukraine specifically.”

巴黎蒙田研究所的布兰奇·勒里东告诉我，欧洲也不能指望新当选的国会民主党人代表其挑战特朗普。“我们必须清醒地认识到，民主党在国会的优先事项不会是重建与欧洲更和谐的关系。在目前阶段，他们的外交政策很难解读。无论是涉及欧洲还是具体涉及乌克兰，更进步的派别和中间派别之间都没有单一、一致的立场。”

**6.** We can’t even assume that nationalist populist parties, encouraged by Trump to destroy the EU from the inside, will suffer by their association with him. True, in central and eastern Europe, where the Maga spirit was heavily promoted by Viktor Orbán, some incumbents may have to perform a pivot. Poland’s conservative president Karol Nawrocki, who is backed by the opposition Eurosceptic Law and Justice party and has vaunted his proximity to Trump, is now facing awkward questions. And in Czechia, which has one of the most pro-Maga governments in Europe, populist PM Andrej Babiš and his far-right coalition partners, the Motorists party, have faced a wave of grassroots protests lately.

我们甚至不能假设，受特朗普鼓励从内部摧毁欧盟的民族主义民粹政党，会因与他的关联而受损。诚然，在中东欧，维克托·欧尔班大力宣扬Maga精神，一些现任者可能不得不转向。波兰保守派总统卡罗尔·纳夫罗茨基由反对派欧洲怀疑主义政党法律与公正党支持，并曾吹嘘自己与特朗普关系密切，现在正面临尴尬的问题。而在拥有欧洲最亲Maga政府之一的捷克，民粹主义总理安德烈·巴比什及其极右翼联盟伙伴“驾车者党”最近面临了一波基层抗议。

**7.** In Italy meanwhile, the far-right prime minister Giorgia Meloni played up tensions with Trump months ago, a strategic move to allay the concerns of voters, the majority of whom despise him.

与此同时在意大利，极右翼总理焦尔吉娅·梅洛尼几个月前渲染了与特朗普的紧张关系，这是一项战略性举措，以缓解选民的担忧，其中大多数人鄙视特朗普。

**8.** But in countries where populists have not yet been in government and are polling at record levels, Trump’s humiliation may not matter to voters, even if their grievances – such as stagnant pay, inflation and lack of affordable housing – are often driven by global forces.

但在民粹主义者尚未执政且民调处于创纪录水平的国家，特朗普的羞辱可能对选民无关紧要，即使他们的不满——如工资停滞、通货膨胀和缺乏负担得起的住房——往往是由全球力量驱动的。

**9.** “It would be wrong to conclude that national-populist and reactionary forces are in retreat everywhere in the west,” Leridon says. “The Democrats won in the US because people were fed up with Trump, in Serbia, [Aleksandar] Vučić was overthrown after 14 years, but in countries where populist political forces are not in power, but on the verge of gaining it, the calculation is different.”

“如果得出结论说民族民粹主义和反动势力在西方各地都在撤退，那将是错误的，”勒里东说。“民主党在美国获胜是因为人们受够了特朗普，在塞尔维亚，[亚历山大]武契奇在14年后被推翻，但在民粹政治力量尚未执政但即将获得政权的国家，情况就不同了。”

**10.** In France, Marine Le Pen is still leading in the polls, six months ahead of a presidential election being fought on cost-of-living issues. Her far-right National Rally party identified Trump as a liability some time ago. “Le Pen and [Jordan] Bardella understood perfectly the radioactive effect the US president could have on them as politicians claiming to defend French national interests. Above all, they recoiled from the corrosive impact of Trump’s policies on purchasing power in France, whether via trade tariffs or the war in Iran,” Leridon says.

在法国，玛丽娜·勒庞在民调中仍然领先，距离以生活成本问题为焦点的总统选举还有六个月。她的极右翼国民联盟党不久前就将特朗普视为一个负担。“勒庞和[乔丹]巴尔代拉完全理解这位美国总统作为声称捍卫法国国家利益的政客可能对他们产生的放射性影响。最重要的是，他们对特朗普政策对法国购买力的侵蚀性影响感到退缩，无论是通过贸易关税还是伊朗战争，”勒里东说。

**11.** In Germany, Alice Weidel, a co-leader of the far-right Alternative für Deutschland, had also distanced the party from Trump in response to tariffs and Greenland. Weidel now blames Trump’s defeat on broken promises about going to war with Iran.

在德国，极右翼德国选择党的联合领导人爱丽丝·魏德尔也因关税和格陵兰问题使该党与特朗普保持距离。魏德尔现在将特朗普的失败归咎于关于与伊朗开战的承诺被打破。

**12.** A critical test for Europe’s political direction is looming in Spain. Socialist prime minister Pedro Sánchez, who called a snap election for 29 November, welcomed Trump’s defeat as a straw in the wind for the revival of progressive forces. But amid public outcry over housing and wages (the cause of Wednesday’s general strike), the momentum is with the opposition rightwing Popular Party (PP) – which could coalesce with the far-right Vox. If this happens, it will be the first time a far-right party enters government in Spain since the end of the Franco dictatorship in 1975. Santiago Abascal, the Vox leader, dismissed any suggestion of an ideological connection with Trump’s Republicans. “We are in Spain, not in Michigan,” he said after the midterms.

欧洲政治方向的关键考验正在西班牙逼近。社会党首相佩德罗·桑切斯呼吁在11月29日提前举行选举，他欢迎特朗普的失败，认为这是进步力量复兴的风向标。但在公众对住房和工资的强烈抗议（周三总罢工的原因）中，势头在反对派右翼人民党（PP）一边——该党可能与极右翼Vox联合。如果发生这种情况，将是自1975年佛朗哥独裁结束以来极右翼政党首次进入西班牙政府。Vox领导人圣地亚哥·阿巴斯卡尔驳斥了任何与特朗普共和党存在意识形态联系的说法。“我们在西班牙，不是在密歇根，”他在中期选举后说。

**13.** A yearning for generational change

对代际变革的渴望

**14.** And yet, longer term, the broader, more hopeful lesson from across the Atlantic could be that after years of global backsliding liberal democracy is not dead. “If Europe can celebrate an essential point, it is that American democracy is alive and was the big winner of these elections,” Leridon says.

然而，从长远来看，来自大西洋彼岸的更广泛、更有希望的教训可能是，在多年的全球倒退之后，自由民主并未死亡。“如果欧洲能庆祝一个关键点，那就是美国民主还活着，并且是这些选举的大赢家，”勒里东说。

**15.** Could this even, as Alexander Hurst asked, be a “democratic spring” – one that began in Budapest in April with Orbán’s defeat? Julian Borger unpacked how a sequence of four pivotal elections this year, in Hungary, Serbia, Brazil and now the US, has shown the power of grassroots activism to produce surprises and could, perhaps, begin the reversal of a worldwide drift towards authoritarianism.

这甚至可能如亚历山大·赫斯特所问，是一场“民主之春”——一场始于四月布达佩斯欧尔班失败的民主之春吗？朱利安·博格解读了今年在匈牙利、塞尔维亚、巴西以及现在的美国接连举行的四场关键选举，如何展示了基层行动主义产生意外结果的力量，或许还可能开始扭转全球向威权主义漂移的趋势。

**16.** The US vote could even shake Europe’s ailing mainstream parties out of their fatalistic mimicking of far-right policies and into action. Gen Z voters fuelled the US blue wave, which showed a yearning for generational change. At a time when youth-led protest movements have rocked Europe, progressive parties may be able to channel protest energy into serious political change. They can’t say they haven’t been warned, loudly, to heed calls for democratic renewal, education, decent housing, equality and limits on the power of the big tech billionaires. Or that failure to do so could allow grassroots anger to be co-opted into the same kind of anti-system nihilism that fuelled Maga.

美国的投票甚至可能促使欧洲病痛缠身的主流政党摆脱对极右政策的宿命论式模仿，采取行动。Z世代选民推动了美国的蓝色浪潮，显示出对代际变革的渴望。在青年领导的抗议运动震撼欧洲之际，进步政党或许能够将抗议能量转化为严肃的政治变革。他们不能说没有受到大声警告，要求他们关注民主复兴、教育、体面住房、平等以及限制大型科技亿万富翁权力的呼声。也不能说如果不这样做，可能会让基层愤怒被吸收为助长Maga的同类反体制虚无主义。
