原文

More than 150 Australian artists, including Hugh Jackman, Rebel Wilson and Jessica Mauboy, have backed a Greenpeace-led campaign against Woodside Energy’s proposal to develop the Browse basin, about 300km off the Kimberley coast.

Woodside, the country’s largest fossil fuel company, describes Browse as “Australia’s biggest undeveloped offshore gas resource”. The oil and gas giant is hoping to win federal government approval to drill more than 50 gas wells about 3km from the Scott Reef area, which is home to more than 1,500 species, including endangered pygmy blue whales.

Actors Jackman, Wilson, Richard Roxburgh and Mia Wasikowska are among prominent Australians who oppose the Browse development on the basis that the proposed gasfield could damage the unique reef ecosystem.

They join musicians Jimmy Barnes, Paul Kelly, Tame Impala’s Kevin Parker, Tina Arena, Jessica Mauboy, G Flip, Vance Joy, Missy Higgins and Baker Boy in signing an open letter calling on the federal environment minister, Murray Watt, not to approve the development.

The letter – which says the Browse project “would turn Scott Reef into an industrial gas zone with decades of deafening seismic blasting [and] carbon dumping beneath the ocean floor” – was also signed by authors JM Coetzee, Helen Garner, Anna Funder and Diana Reid.

“Drilling at Scott Reef could cause an oil spill up to 30 times bigger than the 2009 Montara disaster, polluting Scott Reef with oil condensate, impacting the Kimberley coastline and even reaching as far as Indonesia,” it continued.

The open letter comes after a supergroup of more than 20 Australian artists – including King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, Barnes, John Butler and Montaigne – last Monday released If Not Now, a musical call to arms about the reef.

The song, a reimagining of King Gizzard’s 2020 track, If Not Now, Then When?, is a collaboration between Greenpeace Australia Pacific, Green Music Australia and p(doom) Records.

Browse, first proposed by Woodside in 2018, would see gas piped about 900km to its ageing North West Shelf gas export plant, which is adjacent to the world heritage-listed Murujuga rock art.

Last month, Guardian Australia revealed that WA’s independent environmental watchdog had sharply criticised the proposal, saying that plans to protect turtles and blue whales near Scott Reef would be ineffective.

A draft letter from the Environmental Protection Authority, written in 2023, advised that it may be impossible to reduce the risk of an oil spill to an acceptable level.

Greenpeace last month nominated Scott Reef for national heritage listing, based on its ecological and scientific values.

Modelling by the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis suggests that Browse would generate up to 6.8m tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions yearly at peak production, accounting for between 2.9% and 3.7% of Australia’s emissions in 2035.

“Browse gas is likely four times more expensive than existing domestic gas,” the institute found last year, adding that the project “is unlikely to be competitive either domestically or internationally, based on IEEFA cost estimates”.

Matt Roberts, executive director of the Conservation Council of Western Australia, earlier this month described Woodside’s proposal as “extremely concerning”.

“At the end of the day, we don’t need Browse gas. Western Australia has enough gas available to support us through the transition to a renewable future and regional demand for LNG is declining.

“The federal and state governments have enough evidence to reject this disastrous proposal and save Scott Reef, and we expect them to do just that.”

A spokesperson for the national EPA has told Guardian Australia the development was being assessed under the national Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act, and the “decision clock” had been paused while the agency waited on “further technical advice”.

A Woodside spokesperson said the proposed development “remains subject to a detailed, public and transparent assessment by state and Commonwealth regulators”.

Feedback had helped reduce “the infrastructure footprint around environmentally sensitive areas like Scott Reef – which will not be built on, drilled or mined”, they said.

“Browse is a natural gas and condensate development, not an oil development. References to a potential ‘oil spill’ from Browse are inaccurate.” The spokesperson added that the “longest credible duration of a potential well blowout, while still highly unlikely”, was around 12 hours.

长难句拆解

1. Actors Jackman, Wilson, Richard Roxburgh and Mia Wasikowska are among prominent Australians who oppose the Browse development on the basis that the proposed gasfield could damage the unique reef ecosystem.

结构：定语从句 + 同位语从句（on the basis that...）

讲解：主干是“A are among prominent Australians”，后面 who oppose... 是限制性定语从句修饰 Australians。真正的难点在 on the basis that：这里的 that 引导的是同位语从句，说明 basis 的具体内容，而不是定语从句（that 在从句中不作成分，只起连接作用）。外刊在交代“理由”时很喜欢用 on the basis that / on the grounds that，比 because 更正式、更像“立场陈述”。注意从句里用了 could，是虚拟/推测语气，因为损害尚未发生。

2. The letter – which says the Browse project “would turn Scott Reef into an industrial gas zone with decades of deafening seismic blasting [and] carbon dumping beneath the ocean floor” – was also signed by authors JM Coetzee, Helen Garner, Anna Funder and Diana Reid.

结构：破折号插入语（非限制性定语从句）

讲解：主语 The letter 和谓语 was also signed 之间被一对破折号插入了一个 which 引导的非限制性定语从句，用来补充说明信的内容。这样写的好处是：既把关键信息（信里说了什么）放在最醒目的位置，又不打断主句的“主—谓”逻辑；读者跳过破折号也能读通全句。阅读时遇到成对破折号，可以先把它整体当作“括号”略过，抓住主干再回头补细节。注意从句中 would turn 是转述语气，表示“（信中说）将会把……”。

3. Browse, first proposed by Woodside in 2018, would see gas piped about 900km to its ageing North West Shelf gas export plant, which is adjacent to the world heritage-listed Murujuga rock art.

结构：过去分词后置定语 + see sth done 结构 + 非限制性定语从句

讲解：first proposed by Woodside in 2018 是过去分词短语作后置定语（相当于 which was first proposed...），被逗号隔开插在主语后。谓语用 would see gas piped...，这是 see 的“经历、见证”用法：see + 宾语 + 过去分词，表示“（项目）将使天然气被管道输送……”，比被动句更紧凑，是外刊描述工程、规划时的常用句式。句末 which is adjacent to... 是非限制性定语从句，补充说明厂址位置，暗示项目与敏感地点（世界遗产岩画）距离很近——这是记者不着痕迹的立场表达。

理解题

1. What is the main purpose of this article?

参考答案：报道150多位澳大利亚艺术家、作家和音乐人联名签署公开信，反对伍德赛德能源公司在斯科特礁附近开发Browse气田；同时补充项目背景、监管机构与独立研究机构的质疑，以及伍德赛德方面的回应，让读者了解这场环保争议的全貌。

2. Which side of the dispute does the article provide more supporting evidence for? How can you tell from the structure of the text?
