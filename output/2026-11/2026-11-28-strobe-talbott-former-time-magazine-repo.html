原文

Strobe Talbott, a former Time magazine reporter who later served as deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and then headed the Brookings Institution, died Thursday. He was 80.

His son, Devin Talbott, confirmed he passed away peacefully after a long illness in his East Hampton, New York, home.

The elder Talbott was educated at Yale and Oxford, where he bunked with Clinton. While at the British university, after interning for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked Talbott to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union who was ousted in 1964.

Talbott did, and it gave him some notoriety as a young man and a job at the magazine.

After 21 years at Time, where he focused on Soviet-American relations and held positions including Washington bureau chief and a foreign-affairs columnist, according to his U.S. State Department biography, he received a call from the newly elected Clinton.

Talbott joined the U.S. State Department in 1994, swiftly becoming deputy secretary of state. In that role, he was the architect of the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries and conducted negotiations between the U.S. and India after the latter's nuclear tests in 1998.

"Strobe was a good and brilliant man who made our world more peaceful and secure," said Clinton in a statement after Talbott's death. When Talbott was translating Khrushchev's memoirs while at Oxford, it was Clinton's job to make him breakfast every day, he said, adding, "somehow, I got the better end of the deal."

Both he and Hillary were "deeply saddened" by the passing of "a wonderful friend, and a world-class human being," he said.

Afterward, he served as the president of Brookings from 2002 to 2017. In that time, he also chaired the U.S. State Department's Foreign Affairs Policy Board.

Talbott also authored over a dozen books. His last book was titled "Our Founders' Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump," which was published in 2020.

He was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946, and is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian, and six grandchildren.

His first wife, Brook Shearer, died in 2009, and he later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.

长难句拆解

1. Strobe Talbott, a former Time magazine reporter who later served as deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and then headed the Brookings Institution, died Thursday.

结构：长同位语 + 定语从句（主语与谓语被隔开）

讲解：主语 Strobe Talbott 后面插入了一个同位语 a former Time magazine reporter，同位语里又套了一个 who 引导的定语从句，把“记者—副国务卿—布鲁金斯学会会长”三段履历一次说清，谓语 died Thursday 直到句尾才出现。这是英文讣告和新闻导语的典型写法：名字先行，身份层层展开，最后才给出“死亡”这个核心事件，形成“人名—履历—结局”的信息节奏，也避免了拆成好几个短句。

2. While at the British university, after interning for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked Talbott to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union who was ousted in 1964.

结构：状语从句的省略 + 多重状语前置 + 句末同位语

讲解：While at the British university 是省略形式，完整应为 While he was at the British university（当从句主语与主句主语一致且含 be 动词时可省略主语和 be）。后面 after interning for Time in Moscow 又是一个介词短语状语，两个时间状语叠加，先把时间脉络交代清楚。宾语后面又接了同位语 the former first secretary...，并再次套上 who 引导的定语从句解释赫鲁晓夫的身份。把长修饰成分放在句末，符合英语的“尾重原则”（end weight），句子读起来更稳。

3. "Strobe was a good and brilliant man who made our world more peaceful and secure," said Clinton in a statement after Talbott's death.

结构：引语前置 + 主谓倒装（said Clinton）

讲解：直接引语放在最前面，后面用 said Clinton 而不是 Clinton said，构成主谓倒装。这种“引语 + 动词 + 说话人”的结构在新闻报道中非常常见：读者的注意力先落在引语内容上，再从后半句得到消息来源。注意倒装后仍用名词 Clinton，如果说话人用代词则一般不到装（如 he said），这是英文引语呈现的一条小规律。
