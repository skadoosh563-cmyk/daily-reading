**伊朗公布“我们将杀死特朗普”横幅——但美国总统如今或有意重启轰炸**

## 一、导读

文章报道伊朗在德黑兰市中心悬挂巨幅横幅，以“我们将杀死特朗普”的标语公开羞辱美国总统，并将美国中期选举结果视为对特朗普的“击杀”。伊朗国内强硬派对此充满胜利情绪，但最高领袖同时警告不要指望美国政策转向。与此同时，文章分析了特朗普在中期选举受挫后可能面临的抉择：是在剩余任期内寻求对伊妥协，还是为挽回颜面再度对伊朗动武。文章还涉及中国、海湾国家等外部势力的斡旋动向，整体呈现出美伊对抗进入新阶段的复杂图景。

## 二、原文

Iran this week unveiled a huge billboard and banners over Enqelab Square in central Tehran. The billboard pledged in English: “We will kill Trump” – elevating the degree of personal animus with which Donald Trump is viewed in official Iranian circles.

The poster depicts the US president behind bars with his head bowed. And in a sense, given the US midterm election results on Tuesday, Iran has indeed killed Trump, or at least the second term of his presidency. Trump’s reverse is all the more piquant because it comes on the day Iran’s leadership celebrates the anniversary of the seizure of the US embassy in Tehran in 1979, an event that ensnared Jimmy Carter, and a reminder of how Iran has ruined the prospects of more than one US president.

Not surprisingly, the keenly followed election results have led to a good degree of triumphalism inside Iran.

Ehsan Movaheidian, an international relations scholar, claimed: “The Islamic Republic of Iran has succeeded in regime change in America. The same will soon happen in Europe as well. Bow down before the power of Iran.”

Meisam Zohurian, a conservative member of parliament, said: “The joy over the outcome of the US elections has nothing to do with the arrival of this party or the departure of that party. The issue is the aggressor was smeared in the dirt and Iran had an impact on the result.”

Ali Nikzad, the first deputy speaker of the parliament, said: “These electoral defeats are the aftershocks of Trump’s defeat by the Iranian people.”

Iran’s supreme leader said anyone expecting a shift in US policy was “seriously mistaken”, however. “Those who still harbour even the slightest hope that the behaviour of the hegemonic system might change through partisan or factional shifts, or who imagine that making concessions to the United States can dissuade it from hostility towards the Iranian nation, are seriously mistaken,” Mojtaba Khamenei wrote.

Others predicted further electoral defeats for those they considered to be the warmongers in Europe, and the expulsion of US bases from the entire Middle East.

The resistance – or bloody-mindedness – shown by Iran for the past eight gruelling months did allow the strait of Hormuz to become an Iranian-controlled channel through which inflation and high diesel prices were transmitted to the US system. Even Trump himself on Wednesday accepted, and lamented, that the American people had decided the “short-term” pain of inflation was not a price worth paying to prevent Iran becoming a nuclear power.

But the big question now is whether either side will regard these seismic results as a moment to review their diplomatic strategy. Iran’s chief negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, was quick to call on Trump to accept the wisdom of the American people. On social media he urged Trump to bow to the verdict of America: “Whether driven by conscience or the cost of living, America said no to war and we endorse this message.”

Ghalibaf added: “If the US government betrays the public will in order to serve international paymasters and blackmailers, it will learn our ‘no’ comes with a nightmare.”

Yet Iran will also be asked to reflect on the wisdom of war by the Gulf monarchies, and possibly by Russia and China – the latter a country that finally seems to be stirring itself to find a solution to the blockade in the strait of Hormuz which is preventing badly needed Middle East oil exports from reaching its industries.

China tends to knock only on doors it knows are going to open, so it is probably testing the feasibility of a deal in which Iran “downblends” – or dilutes – its stockpile of highly enriched uranium inside Iran under international supervision. A Chinese special envoy is travelling to Oman, Iran and Saudi Arabia almost immediately.

These countries, some sympathetic to Iran’s predicament, can point out that if Tehran ever thought in the run-up to the elections Trump would capitulate and turn his carrier fleet around, it was mistaken. Some Iranian diplomats had indeed hoped that the prospect of defeat in the midterms, and pressure from within the Republican party, would have led Trump to make concessions to Tehran.

In fact, both sides and the chief mediator, Qatar, clearly did try to use polling day as a lever, but in the end neither retreated. Trump, for once, was true to his word and refused to allow the darkening opinion polls to reduce his determination to keep Iran in his vice. Confined to a holding bay of “neither war nor peace”, Trump’s priority was to keep the oil price as low as possible by repeatedly suggesting a settlement might be imminent.

For the next two months before the new Congress convenes in January – so reducing his budgetary options – Trump has greater latitude, and little accountability. The damage to the Republican party has been done and cannot be undone.

Unsurprisingly the Trump influencers who advocated for the war in the first place are piling on pressure to strike again quickly. Laura Loomer wrote on X: “Time for President Trump to deal with Iran once and for all. Just do it, Nothing to lose now. Finish the job.”

But the credibility of Loomer and her like has been severely damaged: in February, she advised Trump that regime change in Iran – “a little excursion” – could be as easy as knocking over the Maduro regime in Venezuela. Similarly, Benjamin Netanyahu is now touting intelligence showing that Iran is preparing to use chemical weapons. The ease with which the Israeli prime minister conjures up intelligence to justify another assault on Iran may by now have struck the White House.

Hillary Clinton, Trump’s old sparring partner, is one who thinks Trump will probably launch the long-delayed attack. She said: “I think he will feel trapped. He will feel angry and resentful and he will react aggressively.”

Having come so far, and paid so high a political price, Trump may in the next two months try to secure a legacy by finishing the job – however he defines that. He is convinced, as he told Fox News this week, that “Iran has very few missiles and drones left, and even less capacity to build any more”. He crowed: “We are dealing with total strength. We can end it any time.”

Unfortunately, such blithe optimism has been heard before.

## 五、长难句拆解

**1）Trump’s reverse is all the more piquant because it comes on the day Iran’s leadership celebrates the anniversary of the seizure of the US embassy in Tehran in 1979, an event that ensnared Jimmy Carter, and a reminder of how Iran has ruined the prospects of more than one US president.**

- 结构：比较级 + 同位语插入 + 定语从句
- 讲解：all the more piquant 是“比较级 + all the more”结构，表示“更加……”。because 从句后紧跟 an event that... 和 a reminder of... 两个同位语，补充说明“占领使馆”事件的历史意义。这种写法在新闻英语中极常见：先用主句抛出判断，再用同位语层层叠加背景信息，使句子信息密度很高但逻辑仍然清晰。

**2）Confined to a holding bay of “neither war nor peace”, Trump’s priority was to keep the oil price as low as possible by repeatedly suggesting a settlement might be imminent.**

- 结构：过去分词短语作状语 + 比喻性名词短语
- 讲解：Confined to... 是过去分词短语作原因/状态状语，修饰主句主语 Trump。a holding bay 原指机场等待停机位，这里比喻“不战不和”的僵持状态。by repeatedly suggesting 后接省略 that 的宾语从句，是外刊中常见的“以反复表态来管理预期”的写作手法。
