原文

Almost one in five young women and girls in the UK have experienced digital financial products being used as a tool of abuse by a current or former partner, a report has found.

Abusers are exploiting banking apps, online payments and digital credit to control young women, causing financial harm and preventing them from leaving relationships, according to research from the charity Surviving Economic Abuse.

Research for a new report from the charity, Access Denied, Futures Derailed, involved carrying out a nationally representative survey of young women and teenage girls, with Ipsos UK.

It found that almost one in five teenage girls and young women aged 16-24 in the UK – equivalent to nearly 700,000 people – had been affected by this form of abuse. One in three young women experience economic abuse. Rates are highest among disabled young women and those with caring responsibilities for children, with perpetrators exploiting additional vulnerabilities to exert control.

The research found that one in eight girls and young women had experienced a current or ex-partner preventing them from having login information, such as passwords for online banking, while the same number had experienced an abuser controlling their digital wallets.

It also found 7% of respondents had experienced an abuser taking out credit in their name without their consent or because they were scared to refuse, including from “buy now, pay later” lenders.

More than 40% of those who had experienced abuse said it had caused them financial difficulty, while half said it had had a negative impact on their mental health, such as anxiety, depression, panic attacks, self-harm or suicidal thoughts.

More than a third said the economic abuse prevented them from leaving a relationship with the abuser, and one in six had had to borrow money, including from digital lenders such as “buy now, pay later” and online payday lenders, to cover essential items or bills.

Becky* met her now ex-partner when she was 18 and was trapped in a relationship with him for more than a decade, during which time he isolated her from her family and friends, coerced her into more than £40,000 of debt, and prevented her from working.

Even though she fled nearly seven years ago, she continues to live with the impact of his abuse to this day. “I had good credit at the time and he didn’t,” she said. “Even though I said no, he broke me down over many months and forced me to take out a loan in my name to pay for a holiday that he wanted to go on.”

She said that he also obtained “buy now, pay later” loans in her name, which she only found out about when letters came through the post. “There was no verification, it seemed like all he needed from my address and date of birth,” she said, adding that when she left the relationship, the interest on the debt “was huge”.

“I had to access an [individual voluntary arrangement] but this destroyed my credit score,” she said. “When I fled, it was a huge struggle. I couldn’t get a phone or wifi.”

As it was her first proper relationship, she said she “didn’t know what was healthy or not healthy”, adding: “He also isolated me from my family and friends, so I had no one to reach out to. I wish that the government would make sure there is education about economic abuse in schools and in colleges.

“We also need to see banks building in extra layers of protection, particularly with the rising use of AI, they need to be responding to evolving perpetrator tactics.”

Experts and survivors are calling on ministers to use its promised financial education curriculum to educate young people about economic abuse. They are also asking financial services to remove opportunities for domestic abusers to exploit digital financial products and create additional protections to support survivors, including when adopting AI into their systems and processes.

Sam Smethers, the chief executive of Surviving Economic Abuse, said digital financial products could be “weapons of abuse”.

“To halve violence against women and girls, we must tackle abuse where it happens, including through the digital financial products young women use every day,” she added.

“We also want to see financial services firms working with survivors and experts to design out abuse of digital financial products and build in protections. Without action, we risk leaving hundreds of thousands of young women to pay the price.”

The minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls, Satvir Kaur, said: “This report shines an important light on a type of abuse that too often goes unrecognised and can take many forms, from controlling someone’s money, to their choices, to their future.”

“This government has set the unprecedented ambition to halve violence against women and girls in a decade,” Kaur added. “Through our ‘Enough’ campaign, we are working with partners across society, including the banking sector, to help people recognise the signs of economic abuse, challenge abusive behaviour and support victims to access the help they need and deserve.”

长难句拆解

1. It found that almost one in five teenage girls and young women aged 16-24 in the UK – equivalent to nearly 700,000 people – had been affected by this form of abuse.

结构：插入语（破折号补充说明）+ 过去完成时被动语态

讲解：两个破折号之间的“equivalent to nearly 700,000 people”是插入语，用来对前面的“almost one in five”做数量换算，帮助读者直观理解比例对应的实际人数。去掉插入语后，主干是“It found that ... had been affected by this form of abuse”，其中“had been affected”是过去完成时的被动语态，因为研究结果是在调查之后得出的，受影响的动作发生在“found”之前。这种写法在新闻报告中很常见：先用比例给出结论，再用插入语补充具体人数，信息密度高且阅读节奏清晰。

2. More than 40% of those who had experienced abuse said it had caused them financial difficulty, while half said it had had a negative impact on their mental health, such as anxiety, depression, panic attacks, self-harm or suicidal thoughts.

结构：who 引导的定语从句 + while 表对比 + 过去完成时“had had”

讲解：“who had experienced abuse”是定语从句修饰“those”，说明调查对象是“经历过虐待的人”。“while”连接两个并列分句，表示对比：40%多的人说造成经济困难，而一半的人说对心理健康有负面影响。注意“said it had had a negative impact”中连续两个“had”：第一个“had”是“said”的宾语从句中的助动词，构成过去完成时；第二个“had”是实义动词“有”的过去分词。过去完成时“had had”表示“在说（said）之前就已经产生了影响”，时间层次清楚。这种“while + 两个数据对比”的结构在外刊数据类报道中非常典型。

3. Even though she fled nearly seven years ago, she continues to live with the impact of his abuse to this day.

结构：Even though 引导的让步状语从句 + 一般现在时表持续状态

讲解：“Even though”引导让步状语从句，表示“尽管……”，与主句形成转折关系。主句用一般现在时“continues to live with”，强调影响从过去延续到现在，甚至“to this day”（直到今天）。这里没有用过去时，是因为作者要突出虐待的影响具有持续性，而非一次性事件。这种“让步从句 + 现在时”的组合在人物报道中常用来强调受害者长期承受的后果，比单纯用过去时更有感染力。
