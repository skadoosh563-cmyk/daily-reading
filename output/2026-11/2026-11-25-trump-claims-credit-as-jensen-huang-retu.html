原文

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO of the world's largest chipmaker on Monday, sending the company's stock soaring as President Trump claimed credit for brokering his close ally's return.

It was a stunning reversal of fortune for Huang. He resigned less than two weeks ago, following news reports of extramarital affairs, including with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies suspected of being a Chinese spy.

In a statement put out just before the market opened, Nvidia said its board voted unanimously in favor of bringing back Huang after a series of conversations and meetings, as well as input from people at "senior levels" of the U.S. government. "Based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk," the board said.

Nvidia's stock price shot up more than 30% on Monday, helping buoy stock indexes after last week's losses, which were driven by the tech sector.

Trump endorsed the move, calling it the "correct and proper decision" in a social media post. He said Huang's leadership was needed "in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy."

The president had repeatedly urged Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang as CEO in public comments and on social media. In a lengthy interview with CNBC Monday morning, Trump claimed his influence helped Huang get his job back.

"I don't want to say, but basically I was represented at the board," the president said, in response to a question about a report in Semafor that he had called directly into the Nvidia board meeting.

"I spoke to them. Let me just put it that way. And I convinced them," Trump told CNBC. "And I must tell you, it was a lot easier to convince them yesterday than it was a week and a half ago."

He later added: "When the stock starts dropping forty, fifty percent, it was a lot easier for me to do it yesterday than it would have been."

Huang stepped down amid a growing scandal

Nvidia, which Huang co-founded and has run for the past 33 years, dominates the market for the chips used to train and run artificial intelligence. Trump and Huang have grown close during the president's second term in office, with Huang becoming one of the most influential business leaders with the president's ear on AI policy. He has successfully lobbied the U.S. government to allow chipmakers to sell some chips to China, although Nvidia's most advanced chips are subject to export controls.

Huang resigned from Nvidia on Nov. 12 after news reports that he had affairs with multiple women, including an Nvidia executive who reported to him. But the most explosive allegation was that he'd engaged in a relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that was buying Nvidia chips, whom U.S. officials believed had ties to Chinese intelligence.

The Justice Department has said it is conducting a criminal investigation of Megaspeed for potential chip smuggling.

On Monday, the Commerce Department said it is adding Megaspeed to a list of companies barred from buying advanced technology, including some AI chips, without a U.S. government license.

Megaspeed didn't immediately respond to a request for comment. The company previously denied wrongdoing and said that Alice Huang left the company in 2025 and that it is conducting an internal investigation.

Nvidia's board said as recently as Nov. 17 that it did not intend to reinstate Huang, though Huang publicly said he would return if the board allowed it. On Monday, board chair Harvey Jones said in a statement: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers."

The board said its turnaround followed a series of meetings with Huang that left them satisfied with his "candor and ability to lead" the company. It said its investigation into Huang and the reports he had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort continues and that Huang is "committed to cooperating with it."

In a statement on Monday, Huang said he was "honored" to return. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment," he said.

Silicon Valley cheers as questions about Nvidia's governance linger

The board's abrupt reversal was cheered by many Silicon Valley leaders and investors who had joined calls for Huang to return.

"The mistake has been corrected," SpaceX CEO Elon Musk wrote on his social media site X. Gavin Baker, whose firm Atreides Management invests in Nvidia, posted a video of employees applauding Huang as he returned to the chipmaker's headquarters in Santa Clara, Calif. on Monday.

"The idea that this company can only be successful and continue to do what it's doing with that person at the helm ... is the argument made by a lot in Silicon Valley last week and by the president — that Nvidia's success depends on Jensen running it," said Margaret O'Mara, a historian who studies Silicon Valley at the University of Washington.

Some women who have advocated for the tech industry to tackle gender inequality and abuses of power say that for the board to reinstate Huang after previously saying his behavior was "inconsistent" with the company's code of conduct sends a signal to Silicon Valley.

"It is a resounding message that all that matters is money," said Freada Kapor Klein, a social impact investor who's advocated for women and people of color in tech.

Nvidia did not respond to NPR's questions about those concerns.

Huang's reinstatement also raises questions about Nvidia's corporate governance and how the board is weighing short-term considerations against the company's policies, said Rob Lalka, a professor at Tulane University's Freeman School of Business.

"It really was sort of an eye-opening moment to think about how this board, just a few days ago, had said they would not bring Jensen Huang back and that they had voted unanimously for that. There's still an investigation open in their company" into Huang's behavior, Lalka said. "And the board basically went around its entire process that it had set in place for that investigation to put him back into power."

Trump dismisses national security concerns

On Monday, Trump wrote on Truth Social that the government has "no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security," and that Attorney General Todd Blanche says the U.S. "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

Last week Nvidia's board said it had formed a committee to oversee the company's national security policies, compliance with U.S. export controls, and risk management of global trade.

Huang is expected to testify before multiple congressional committees next week.

To explain why it was so important that Huang remain at Nvidia's helm, Trump told CNBC he feared China attempting to bring his expertise to their country.

"I could see China coming in and saying to Jensen, 'Hey, how would you like to come to China?' He's got a little blood in him, you know. He's got a little blood there. 'How would you like to come to China and head up Huawei, okay, and head up one of our companies?'" Trump said. "And you know what? If Jensen was angry enough, if he was treated badly, maybe he could do that."

When CNBC asked Trump if he wasn't worried after he was alerted that Huang was in a relationship with a potential spy, Trump demurred, saying, "I won't get into it."

Following the CNBC interview, Trump celebrated Huang's reinstatement on Truth Social by posting a doctored image invoking a famous photo of the president after the assassination attempt in Butler, Penn., in 2024. In the posted image, Huang is in President Trump's place with blood spattered on his face, raising his fist — holding a red bra — as a flag bearing Nvidia's logo waves in the background.

NPR's Andrea Hsu contributed to this report.
